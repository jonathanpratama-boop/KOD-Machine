## Fixed trading rules removed

| Old fixed rule | Replaced by |
|---|---|
| Buy every alert at the next hour | Each candidate is judged: **preferred**, **watch** or **no action** |
| +50% target, 14-day hold, every time | Target and holding time decided per candidate, from the chart, the catalyst and the crowd mood |
| Same entry style in every market | Entry chosen case by case, with a note of how many winners that style has historically skipped |
| Six chart clues added up as a score | Different kinds of evidence weighed separately; look-alike chart clues count once |
| "Only trade in Greed" switch | Mood guides how long to hold, not whether to trade |

The clue ledger becomes a reference: each clue is labelled **passed**, **clue only** or **failed**. KOD can go against a
clue, but it has to say why.

## Kept (these are not trading rules; without them good judgment cannot be told from luck)

1. **Write the decision down before the outcome.** The pick, the reasoning, the entry plan and the rejected names are
   recorded with a timestamp. "No action" counts as a decision.
2. **Keep the scoring fixed.** Each decision is compared, after costs, with a chart-only ranking of the same candidates,
   a random pick, holding Bitcoin, and cash. The first verdict comes after 60 decisions.
3. **Paper only.** No real money without the owner's approval of a specific trade.
4. **A few evidence-backed limits:** no leverage, no shorting when shorts are already crowded, and costs always counted.

## Not removed

- The past tests and their results (the evidence behind the clues).
- The live tests already running on GitHub (changing them mid-run would void them).

**Caveat (Hermes):** judgment cannot be tested on past data, because an AI may already know what happened, so the only
proof is live, and it is slow. That is why the record-and-score rules stay fixed.
